Forecasting U.S. Business Insurance Premium Prices

Monthly model-driven forecasts of the national index of business insurance premium prices, built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

+0.155%
Predicted MoM change in U.S. business insurance premium prices for Nov 2026

U.S. business insurance premium prices are forecast to rise approximately +0.155% month-over-month in November 2026, adding roughly $777,000 to a $500 million premium book. The move is modest in isolation but sits within a sustained upward trajectory now spanning more than a year, with pressure distributed across wealth-driven capital positioning, imported equipment and textile costs, and middle-market credit expansion — not concentrated in any single line. Reinsurance treaty calibration, reserve margin discipline, rate lock windows, and capital deployment each carry a share of that $777,000, and the compounding effect of getting all four right outweighs optimizing any one in isolation. Carriers that treat November as a maintenance month rather than a calibration moment risk ceding ground on a trajectory that has proven durable across multiple market phases.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for insurance carrier C-suite, grounded in the current forecast and market conditions. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Reinsurance

CRO / CFO
November's +0.155% move is incremental, but it extends a sustained upward trend that reinsurance panels pricing off trailing-six-month averages may still be discounting. Confirm that year-end treaty renewals — particularly on property and equipment-intensive lines — reflect the full cumulative trajectory rather than the muted pace of any single recent month.

Rate Locks

CFO / Treasury
With roughly $777,000 of incremental premium at stake on a $500 million book, the cost of holding standard lock windows is low and the cost of compressing them further is lower still — maintain existing 15-to-20-day lock discipline without tightening. The more important action is ensuring that November's modest reading does not become internal justification for extending lock windows on large commercial renewals heading into December.

Reserves

CFO / Chief Actuary
Rising import prices for textile furnishings, agricultural and construction machinery, and electrical components are broadening the inflationary base for property and equipment-related loss costs even as the monthly premium move stays measured. Verify that loss development factors on property and inland marine lines reflect current import cost levels before year-end close, and hold any margin additions made in Q3 rather than releasing them on a single month of modest pricing.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A thirteenth month of sustained upward premium pricing — with directional accuracy on 12 of 13 prior calls — supports maintaining current commercial lines deployment posture through year-end rather than rotating defensively. Use November's measured pace to rebalance within the portfolio toward segments with meaningful equipment, textile, and middle-market credit exposure, where the broadest November signals are converging.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Wealth Concentration & Capital Positioning

mixed signal
Upper-wealth-tier households are expanding total liabilities, specialty lending, and deposit holdings — patterns that historically accompany reduced appetite for absorbing insurance and reinsurance risk, supporting firmer primary market pricing. Partially offsetting this, a rising share of money market fund assets held by the same wealth tier signals some rotation back toward liquid, low-risk positions that could gradually restore alternative capacity and temper the upward push.

Middle-Market Credit Expansion

pushing forecast up
Loan growth among middle-wealth households is expanding the insurable base of business assets and commercial property, adding fresh exposure that carriers are writing at current — rather than legacy — rate levels. Simultaneously, a declining share of mortgage holdings in this wealth tier suggests capital is rotating toward other asset classes, modestly constraining the private capacity available to absorb commercial risk.

Imported Equipment & Materials Costs

pushing forecast up
Import prices for agricultural, construction, and mining machinery — as well as electrical equipment and components — have been firming, raising replacement and repair costs for commercial property, heavy equipment, and technology-adjacent claims. These upstream cost pressures broaden the inflationary base supporting premium increases beyond consumer-facing categories and are particularly relevant for property, inland marine, and contractors' equipment lines.

Textile & Furnishings Import Inflation

pushing forecast up
Rising import prices for textile furnishings are adding to commercial property contents valuation and hospitality-sector replacement costs, supporting upward pressure on property and business interruption premium pricing. This signal is narrower in scope than equipment costs but consistent in direction, reinforcing the breadth of the inflationary base heading into year-end renewals.

Labor Market in Professional Occupations

pushing forecast down
The unemployment rate among professional and related occupations has been edging lower, signaling tightening labor conditions that could elevate claims settlement and adjustment expenses over time — but in the near term, a well-employed professional workforce also supports stable insurable exposure and reduces the frequency of certain liability-related loss events. This acts as a modest counterweight to the broader upward premium trajectory.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Raw monthly delta of WPU411 (Insurance PPI) as published by BLS — not interpolated, with a trailing-5-year seasonal mean removed before training and added back at prediction.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.
