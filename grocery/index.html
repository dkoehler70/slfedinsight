Forecasting Real Per-Capita U.S. Grocery Spending

Monthly model-driven forecasts of the month-over-month change in real (inflation-adjusted) per-capita grocery-store spending — how much the average American's grocery dollar is actually moving — built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

+0.010%
Predicted MoM change in real per-capita grocery-store spending (month-over-month % change, inflation-adjusted) for Oct 2026

Real per-capita grocery-store spending is forecast to inch forward in October, with a predicted month-over-month gain of +0.010% — translating to roughly $50,000 on a $500M annual sales base. The signal is positive but thin: downward pressure from import costs, cereal and bakery inflation, and regulatory uncertainty is nearly overwhelming any upward momentum, leaving the net read just above flat. Across merchandising, category mix, promotions, and capital decisions, the practical implication is the same as the number suggests — stay disciplined, avoid over-rotating, and treat this as a consolidation month rather than a growth inflection.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for grocery and food-retail C-suite, grounded in the current grocery-share forecast. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Merchandising & Pricing

VP Merchandising / CFO
A forecast this close to flat calls for surgical pricing rather than broad moves — hold the line on shelf prices in high-frequency staples and resist the temptation to chase volume with category-wide markdowns. Protect gross margin where you have it; the macro environment is not delivering incremental traffic to absorb a margin give-back.

Category Mix

VP Merchandising / VP Strategy
Rising cereal and bakery prices are compressing real value in a core center-store segment — use this window to reinforce private-label positioning in grains and baked goods before shoppers begin substituting down on their own terms. Shift assortment emphasis incrementally toward value-tier SKUs in import-exposed categories where cost pressure is most visible to the shopper.

Promotions & Loyalty

CMO / VP Marketing
With spending growth barely above zero, retention economics outperform acquisition economics by a wide margin — concentrate loyalty investment on deepening basket size with existing households rather than funding broad reach campaigns. Targeted replenishment offers on staples most affected by price pressure will sustain visit frequency without requiring deep discounts.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A near-flat real spending forecast does not provide the demand signal needed to justify accelerating new square footage or major remodel commitments. Maintain current capital pacing and preserve optionality for when the trajectory produces a clearer directional move.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Import Cost Pressure

pushing forecast down
Import prices across a broad range of manufactured goods — industrial equipment, electronics, and machinery from key trading partners — are elevated, raising input costs throughout the supply chain. When the landed cost of goods rises, real purchasing power at the grocery level erodes, putting quiet downward pressure on inflation-adjusted spending volumes.

Cereal & Bakery Inflation

pushing forecast down
Consumer prices for cereals and bakery products are running above the broader food-at-home average, directly compressing the real volume that a given household budget can support in one of grocery's highest-frequency center-store segments. Shoppers absorbing higher bread and cereal prices have less room for incremental spending elsewhere in the store.

Regulatory & Policy Uncertainty

pushing forecast down
Equity market volatility tied to regulatory developments and broader geopolitical uncertainty is weighing on consumer and business confidence. Historically, elevated policy uncertainty correlates with more cautious household spending behavior, including a tendency to defer non-essential grocery purchases and stick to tighter shopping lists.

Upper-Tier Household Credit

pushing forecast down
Loan and advance activity among upper-income households is signaling a modest tightening in discretionary financial behavior at the top of the wealth distribution. When higher-income consumers pull back on credit-financed spending, the ripple effect on premium and convenience grocery formats can be a quiet drag on overall real per-capita volumes.

Non-Manufactured Commodity Imports

pushing forecast down
Rising import volumes of non-manufactured commodities — raw agricultural and natural resource inputs — are adding to cost-side complexity for food retailers and their suppliers. The net effect is modest but consistent with the broader theme of cost pressure outpacing consumer budget growth in the near term.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Month-over-month % change in real per-capita grocery-store spending — grocery-store retail sales (U.S. Census MARTS, seasonally adjusted) deflated by the CPI for food at home (BLS, SA) and divided by U.S. population. Because the underlying sales series is already seasonally adjusted, the model trains on the change directly; the COVID demand spike (2020–21) is excluded from training as a structural outlier.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.

About

Purpose

Proof-of-concept research site forecasting real per-capita U.S. grocery spending for use by grocery and food-retail strategy and merchandising teams. Sister product to the Insurance and Home Builders Editions.

Data Source

The target combines grocery-store retail sales (U.S. Census MARTS), the CPI for food at home (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics) and U.S. population, all sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). To avoid circularity, the target's own components are excluded from the model's candidate predictors.
