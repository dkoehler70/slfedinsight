Forecasting Grocery's Share of the U.S. Food Dollar

Monthly model-driven forecasts of grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending — how the food dollar splits between the grocery aisle and everywhere else — built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

+0.546%
Predicted MoM change in grocery stores' share of U.S. grocery-plus-restaurant spending for Sep 2026

Grocery stores' share of U.S. grocery-plus-restaurant spending is forecast to rise 0.55% month-over-month in September, a tailwind worth roughly $2.7M on a $500M annual grocery sales base. The signal points to consumers leaning back toward the grocery aisle as labor market softness and modest GDP momentum shift spending patterns. Across merchandising, category mix, and loyalty levers, the opportunity is real but sized for disciplined execution — not a broad-based reset of strategy.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for grocery and food-retail C-suite, grounded in the current grocery-share forecast. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Merchandising & Pricing

VP Merchandising / CFO
A share gain of this magnitude supports selective price investment in high-traffic categories rather than a storewide markdown. Hold shelf pricing discipline and direct any promotional spend toward items that drive basket size, not just trip count.

Category Mix

VP Merchandising / VP Strategy
Fresh proteins, produce, and prepared-at-home staples are the likely beneficiaries as consumers trade restaurant meals for home cooking. Ensure in-stock depth and favorable shelf placement in these categories heading into September before the shift fully materializes.

Promotions & Loyalty

CMO / VP Marketing
Use loyalty offers to convert the trade-down shopper into a repeat visitor rather than a one-time deal-seeker. A targeted earn-and-redeem structure on fresh and center-store staples is well-calibrated to the size of this forecast.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A ~$2.7M demand tailwind on a $500M base supports current operating plans but does not justify accelerating capital deployment. Hold expansion pacing steady and treat the share gain as margin protection, not a trigger for incremental store investment.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Labor Market Softening

pushing forecast up
Rising unemployment in service-sector industries historically nudges budget-conscious households away from restaurant spending and toward grocery. Softer employment conditions heading into September are a meaningful tailwind for grocery's share of the food dollar.

Wage Growth Pressure

pushing forecast down
Hourly earnings across private-sector and transportation workers remain elevated, sustaining some consumer purchasing power that keeps restaurant spending competitive. Stronger wages partially offset the trade-down impulse, tempering the size of the share shift.

Broader Economic Momentum

pushing forecast up
Modest real GDP growth supports overall consumer spending volume, and historically a portion of that incremental spending flows disproportionately into grocery as households prioritize value. The GDP signal is a mild but consistent positive for grocery share.

Interest Rates & Financial Conditions

mixed signal
Short-term Treasury rates at current levels create a slight tilt toward grocery by tightening household credit conditions and reducing discretionary restaurant frequency. The effect is modest and works in the background rather than as a primary driver.

Agricultural Policy Uncertainty

pushing forecast up
Elevated volatility in agricultural policy expectations can shift consumer and retailer behavior toward stocking up at grocery rather than dining out, providing a marginal lift to grocery's share. The signal is small but directionally consistent with the overall forecast.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Monthly delta of grocery stores' share of grocery-plus-restaurant spending — grocery-store retail sales divided by grocery-store plus food-service retail sales, from the U.S. Census Bureau Monthly Retail Trade Survey (MARTS). A trailing-5-year seasonal mean is removed before training and added back at prediction.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.

About

Purpose

Proof-of-concept research site forecasting grocery stores' share of U.S. food spending for use by grocery and food-retail strategy and merchandising teams. Sister product to the Insurance and Home Builders Editions.

Data Source

The target is built from the U.S. Census Bureau Monthly Retail Trade Survey (grocery-store retail sales as a share of grocery plus food-service retail sales). All series, including the target components, are sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).
