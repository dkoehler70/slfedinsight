Forecasting Grocery's Share of the U.S. Food Dollar

Monthly model-driven forecasts of grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending — how the food dollar splits between the grocery aisle and everywhere else — built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

-0.380%
Predicted MoM change in grocery stores' share of U.S. grocery-plus-restaurant spending for Sep 2026

Grocery stores' share of U.S. grocery-plus-restaurant spending is forecast to slip 0.38% month-over-month in September — a headwind worth roughly $1.9M on a $500M annual grocery sales base. The pressure is broad-based: policy uncertainty, protein price dynamics, and a modest tilt back toward restaurant spending are all working against the grocery aisle simultaneously. The implication isn't a crisis, but it does call for disciplined category management, targeted loyalty investment, and a steady hand on capital — not a defensive overreaction. Executives who protect margin and sharpen trip-driving promotions now will be better positioned when the share pendulum swings back.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for grocery and food-retail C-suite, grounded in the current grocery-share forecast. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Merchandising & Pricing

VP Merchandising / CFO
A sub-0.4% share dip doesn't justify broad price cuts, but it does warrant tightening promotional discipline on high-velocity SKUs where margin is already thin. Review shelf pricing on proteins specifically — beef and pork cost pressures are a signal to manage mix, not absorb silently.

Category Mix

VP Merchandising / VP Strategy
With protein prices elevated, steer promotional weight toward value-per-serving categories — poultry, plant-based, and center-store meal solutions — that keep the at-home meal proposition compelling. A modest share softening is the moment to reinforce the value narrative before restaurant operators sharpen their own offers.

Promotions & Loyalty

CMO / VP Marketing
Activate loyalty levers now to defend trip frequency against a slight gravitational pull back toward dining out. A targeted earn-and-redeem campaign on weekly staples costs less than recovering a lapsed shopper in October.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A forecast decline of this magnitude — roughly $1.9M in share-driven revenue exposure on a $500M base — supports holding current capital pacing rather than accelerating or pulling back. Revisit expansion timing in Q4 when the trajectory becomes clearer.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Policy & Trade Uncertainty

pushing forecast down
Elevated uncertainty around agricultural and trade policy — particularly U.S.-China trade tensions — is weighing on the grocery share outlook. When policy risk is high, supply-chain costs and import price volatility tend to compress grocery operators' pricing flexibility relative to restaurants.

Protein Price Pressure

pushing forecast down
Rising retail prices for beef and elevated global pork costs are nudging budget-sensitive shoppers to reconsider the at-home meal value equation. Higher protein prices at the grocery shelf can soften the cost advantage that typically pulls consumers away from restaurants.

Financial Market Signals

mixed signal
Utility equity performance — a traditional defensive-sentiment indicator — is providing a modest upward signal, suggesting some consumer caution that historically supports grocery share. Broader macroeconomic and national security policy volatility in equity markets adds a secondary upward nudge, though neither signal is strong enough to offset the downward drivers this month.

Business Formation & Regional Activity

pushing forecast up
New business formation in the West Census Region is running at a pace that historically correlates with incremental grocery spending — more new enterprises means more working households buying food at home. This is a mild positive for grocery share but insufficient to reverse the headline direction.

Restaurant Competitive Dynamics

pushing forecast down
The underlying share data itself signals that restaurants have been recapturing ground in recent months, and September's forecast reflects a continuation of that drift. Without a meaningful shock to away-from-home prices or consumer income, the tilt back toward restaurant spending is expected to persist modestly through the forecast window.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Monthly delta of grocery stores' share of grocery-plus-restaurant spending — grocery-store retail sales divided by grocery-store plus food-service retail sales, from the U.S. Census Bureau Monthly Retail Trade Survey (MARTS). A trailing-5-year seasonal mean is removed before training and added back at prediction.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.

About

Purpose

Proof-of-concept research site forecasting grocery stores' share of U.S. food spending for use by grocery and food-retail strategy and merchandising teams. Sister product to the Insurance and Home Builders Editions.

Data Source

The target is built from the U.S. Census Bureau Monthly Retail Trade Survey (grocery-store retail sales as a share of grocery plus food-service retail sales). All series, including the target components, are sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).
