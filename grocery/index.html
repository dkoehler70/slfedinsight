Forecasting Grocery's Share of the U.S. Food Dollar

Monthly model-driven forecasts of grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending — how the food dollar splits between the grocery aisle and everywhere else — built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

+1.433%
Predicted MoM change in grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending for Sep 2026

Grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending is forecast to rise +1.4% month-over-month in September — a meaningful shift worth roughly $7.2M on a $500M annual grocery sales base. The signal is broad-based: restaurant spending momentum, import cost pressures, and trade uncertainty are all tilting the food dollar back toward the grocery aisle simultaneously. Across merchandising, category mix, promotions, and capital decisions, the size of this move warrants active positioning rather than a wait-and-see stance. Operators who move early on the levers below stand to capture the bulk of the incremental spend before competitors reprice.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for grocery and food-retail C-suite, grounded in the current grocery-share forecast. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Merchandising & Pricing

VP Merchandising / CFO
A +1.4% share shift of this magnitude creates real pricing room — resist the reflex to discount broadly and instead hold price on high-velocity categories where demand is pulling in organically. Tighten shelf assortment around import-exposed SKUs where cost pressures may compress margin, and flag those categories for early Q4 price-architecture review.

Category Mix

VP Merchandising / VP Strategy
Trade-down dynamics and elevated away-from-home costs are steering shoppers toward proteins, fresh produce, and scratch-cooking staples — weight your planogram and endcap space accordingly for September sets. Categories with import exposure (electronics accessories, specialty packaged goods with Pacific Rim inputs) warrant a secondary look at private-label substitution to protect mix margin.

Promotions & Loyalty

CMO / VP Marketing
The trade-down shopper arriving in September is budget-motivated but not yet loyal — structure loyalty offers around basket-building (meal-solution bundles, multi-week pantry loads) rather than single-item discounts that train one-time deal behavior. Targeted digital offers tied to fresh and center-store staples will convert the incremental trip into a repeatable routine before restaurant chains respond with value promotions.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A forecasted share gain of this size supports accelerating near-term investments in store-level execution — labor hours, in-stock position, and checkout capacity — rather than deferring to Q4. Longer-horizon capital commitments (new builds, major remodels) should remain on current pacing; one strong month is a tailwind, not a structural reset.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Restaurant Spending Pressure

pushing forecast up
Away-from-home food sales remain elevated in nominal terms, reflecting persistent restaurant price inflation that is pricing budget-conscious consumers out of frequent dining. As the gap between eating out and eating at home widens, grocery stores capture a larger share of the food dollar — the strongest single tailwind in the September outlook.

Import Costs & Trade Uncertainty

pushing forecast up
Rising import prices on manufactured goods from Asia and elevated trade policy uncertainty are compressing household discretionary budgets, nudging consumers to economize on food by shifting spend toward grocery. Historically, periods of heightened trade friction correlate with a modest but consistent rotation into at-home food preparation.

Healthcare & Non-Food Cost Inflation

pushing forecast up
Medical care commodity prices are trending upward, squeezing the share of wallet available for discretionary spending including restaurant meals. When non-food cost burdens rise, grocery — as the lower-cost food channel — tends to benefit on the margin.

Wage & Income Dynamics

pushing forecast down
Hourly earnings growth in certain sectors remains firm, which historically supports consumer confidence and restaurant frequency — a mild headwind to grocery's share gain. If real wage growth broadens, some of the trade-down impulse could ease before September's numbers fully materialize.

Financial Market Stability

mixed signal
Equity market volatility tied to inflation news and emerging-market uncertainty is running at moderate levels — enough to keep consumer sentiment cautious but not so elevated as to trigger a sharp retrenchment in spending. Cautious sentiment generally favors grocery over restaurants on a 30-60 day lag, adding a secondary lift to the forecast.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Monthly delta of grocery stores' share of total U.S. food spending — grocery-store food sales divided by total food sales (at-home plus away-from-home), from the USDA Food Expenditure Series. A trailing-5-year seasonal mean is removed before training and added back at prediction.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.
