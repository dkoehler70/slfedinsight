Forecasting Real Per-Capita U.S. Grocery Spending

Monthly model-driven forecasts of the month-over-month change in real (inflation-adjusted) per-capita grocery-store spending — how much the average American's grocery dollar is actually moving — built from public economic data.

Latest Forecast

+0.003%
Predicted MoM change in real per-capita grocery-store spending (month-over-month % change, inflation-adjusted) for Sep 2026

Real per-capita grocery-store spending is forecast to be essentially flat in September, up a nominal +0.003% month-over-month — roughly $12,500 on a $500M annual sales base. The signal is not a call to action on volume; it is a call to precision. With spending growth stalled, the margin you protect on pricing, the mix you engineer toward higher-contribution categories, and the loyalty investment you calibrate carefully will collectively determine whether September is a hold or a quiet give-back. At this forecast magnitude, operational discipline across those levers matters more than any single promotional push.

Executive Recommendations

Forward-looking guidance for grocery and food-retail C-suite, grounded in the current grocery-share forecast. Refreshed each weekly model run.

Merchandising & Pricing

VP Merchandising / CFO
With real per-capita spending essentially flat, avoid broad price investments that compress margin without driving incremental trips. Hold shelf pricing steady and let cost discipline — not promotional depth — protect the bottom line this month.

Category Mix

VP Merchandising / VP Strategy
Flat overall spending puts a premium on mix: shift assortment emphasis toward higher-margin fresh, private-label, and prepared-at-home staples where basket contribution holds up even when trip frequency does not. Resist the temptation to fill volume gaps with low-margin commodity items.

Promotions & Loyalty

CMO / VP Marketing
A near-zero growth environment rewards precision over reach — target loyalty offers at your highest-frequency households to defend visit cadence rather than broadcasting broad discounts that erode margin without building retention. Keep promotional spend lean and ROI-gated this cycle.

Capital Allocation

CEO / CFO
A flat spending forecast supports a steady-state capital posture — no case for accelerating expansion or major format investment on the basis of September demand alone. Redirect any discretionary capex toward efficiency and supply-chain resilience rather than footprint growth.

Current Driving Factors

Broad economic categories currently feeding the forecast. Each refreshes when the model retrains.

Broad Producer & Import Prices

pushing forecast down
Upstream cost pressures — spanning producer prices for final goods and import prices across multiple manufacturing categories — are running at levels that historically correlate with softer real consumer spending at the grocery level, as cost pass-through compresses purchasing power without a commensurate income offset.

Energy & Commodity Costs

pushing forecast down
Elevated extraction and energy-related import prices from industrialized-country suppliers add a persistent cost layer to the supply chain; when these prices rise, real inflation-adjusted grocery spending tends to soften as household budgets absorb higher utility and fuel costs alongside food bills.

Consumer Goods Prices

mixed signal
Non-shelter consumer prices are exerting mild downward pressure on real grocery spending, while prices for video and audio products — a proxy for discretionary electronics deflation — provide a small offsetting lift as households retain slightly more spending capacity when durable goods get cheaper.

Business Formation & Regional Activity

pushing forecast down
Slowing high-propensity business applications in the West Census Region suggest a cooling in local economic dynamism; historically, reduced entrepreneurial activity in a region correlates with softer household income growth and, with a lag, restrained grocery spending.

Wealth Concentration & Corporate Retained Earnings

mixed signal
A modest uptick in corporate retained earnings provides a faint positive signal — firms holding earnings can support employment and wages — but the share of financial assets concentrated in upper-wealth households offers little direct stimulus to the broad grocery-spending base, leaving the net effect nearly neutral.

Methodology

Target Variable

DEPVAR
Month-over-month % change in real per-capita grocery-store spending — grocery-store retail sales (U.S. Census MARTS, seasonally adjusted) deflated by the CPI for food at home (BLS, SA) and divided by U.S. population. Because the underlying sales series is already seasonally adjusted, the model trains on the change directly; the COVID demand spike (2020–21) is excluded from training as a structural outlier.

Feature Universe

~21,000 series-derivative features
Constructed from FRED economic data: trailing values, differences, and percent changes at intervals of 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 days.

Modeling

SwarmGA — multi-agent genetic search
Pre-selects 200 candidate features via random forest importance + univariate scoring; a swarm of agents searches feature subsets to maximize a held-out fitness metric.

Backtesting Protocol

Walk-forward, no future leakage
Each model is trained only on data available before the target date. Forward features are constructed from the cutoff and shifted by the forecast horizon.

About

Purpose

Proof-of-concept research site forecasting real per-capita U.S. grocery spending for use by grocery and food-retail strategy and merchandising teams. Sister product to the Insurance and Home Builders Editions.

Data Source

The target combines grocery-store retail sales (U.S. Census MARTS), the CPI for food at home (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics) and U.S. population, all sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). To avoid circularity, the target's own components are excluded from the model's candidate predictors.
